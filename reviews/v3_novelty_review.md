# V3 novelty and literature review

**Search date:** 2026-10-04. **Purpose:** scope the V3 question against primary research on grounding reliability and candidate-set changes. This is a targeted, reproducible search note, not a systematic review or a claim of priority.

## Question and comparison dimensions

V3 evaluates correctness-ranking reliability for a fixed grounding scorer as the number of available object proposals changes. Its controlled comparison uses nested candidate prefixes at requested sizes K=5, 10, 20, and 50, holds proposal identities and scorer fixed across K, and tests effects on positive, compositionally demanding REC. The planned reliability outputs include correctness AUROC and selective-risk measures. The feature-source comparison is separately predeclared: the existing B0 score and feature groups S, S+Q, S+V, Full, plus the B/16 replication; the V3 protocol does not fit a FineCops reliability model.

The word “size” is ambiguous in nearby papers. This review keeps distinct: (a) the number of selectable object/region proposals; (b) the number of stochastic model samples used to estimate spatial uncertainty; (c) the number of generated predictions accumulated over rollouts; and (d) the number of ground-truth targets or output boxes. A reported effect of (b), (c), or (d) is relevant, but it is not automatically an intervention on (a).

A cross-task control was included for [Certified Error Control of Candidate Set Pruning](https://aclanthology.org/2022.emnlp-main.23/), an information-retrieval rather than vision paper.

**Inclusion:** original paper or official proceedings/full-text record; visual grounding, REC, or a clearly marked adjacent candidate-ranking problem; and at least one of confidence, uncertainty, correctness ranking, abstention, reliability, proposal-set size, or difficult compositional REC. **Exclusion:** surveys and secondary commentary; pages that merely restate abstracts where the body was needed to determine candidate-size behavior; generic LLM verbal-confidence work without localization; and unrelated scores with neither a candidate pool nor a grounding correctness target. The one IR paper is retained only as a clearly out-of-domain design analogy.

**Reading depth:** `A` = official abstract/metadata only; `B` = selected body sections, equations/tables/appendix named below; `B+` = multiple body sections plus the candidate-size/reliability result checked. “B” does not imply every page, supplement, code, or reference was audited. The candidate-size judgment is scoped to the named sections and version, not inferred from the title.

The targeted extension deliberately covered two branches underrepresented in generic confidence queries: candidate-pool composition/redundant boxes, and where a grounding feature is sourced from query-to-candidate matching versus candidate-to-candidate context. It surfaced primary REC work on expression-aware NMS and multi-order relation graphs. These papers establish that both candidate composition and cross-candidate features are longstanding grounding design axes; the inspected results do not test correctness-reliability changes under a controlled candidate-count sweep. Several searches returned general grounding architectures or non-REC proposal work only, which were not promoted into detailed claims.

## Interpretation and novelty boundary

The closest comparisons are not empty. SafeGround changes the number of stochastic spatial samples and measures correctness discrimination and risk; MTLA enlarges a generated prediction pool with repeated rollouts and demonstrates confidence-based re-ranking as pool size grows. Both should be cited when describing V3's set-size question. Neither inspected study is a direct synonym for an external proposal-set-size shift: SafeGround's K is repeated-output sampling, while MTLA's N changes the generation pool and reports accuracy gains under re-ranking. The inspected MTLA sections do not show reliability AUROC recalculated for every N. The papers therefore overlap in mechanism and evaluative purpose, even though the exact estimand differs.

REC work also now treats negative rejection, prediction multiplicity, and composition directly. OpenRef's N3R and multi-target/none-target cases, VIRO's verifier-based abstention, RefEgo's target absence, and FineCops-Ref's compositional difficulty levels constrain any claim that this project is the first to study “grounding reliability” or “compositional grounding.” The narrower question is how a *fixed candidate scorer's correctness-ranking behavior changes when an exogenously controlled, nested object proposal set grows*, and whether this pattern is studied on compositionally harder positive REC examples with separately sourced feature families.

Candidate composition and feature provenance are also not new in REC. Ref-NMS changes the retained proposal pool through expression-aware suppression, while CMRIN and Variational Context explicitly use context across proposal pairs. V3's candidate-count axis therefore should not be sold as the first account of proposal-pool effects, and its V feature family should not be described as the first candidate-context representation. The narrower contribution is a reliability estimand under nested candidate prefixes, with source families compared on a fixed scorer/cohort. Its Q family is query-to-crop/alternate-scoring information; its V family summarizes candidate visual similarity and density. The original 16-D semantic block mixes these sources, which is why S, S+Q, S+V, and Full comparisons are needed for attribution. The papers above evaluate grounding accuracy or proposal recall, not that source-isolated reliability comparison.

The targeted search did not identify a primary paper matching that entire combination in the sections screened. This is an observation about this search, not a first-ever claim and not proof of novelty. The neighboring work is sufficiently close that the V3 paper should lead with its precise estimand and compare to candidate-pool and sample-count interventions explicitly. A broad priority claim would require citation chasing across more proceedings, thesis/preprint repositories, and search-index variants.

For V3, proposal-count and sample-count are kept separate in definitions and discussion. A higher K may alter score distributions through the addition of harder distractors even when feature code and scorer stay fixed; it is not the same intervention as increasing Monte Carlo samples, model-generated detections, ground-truth target count, or RPN recall. The B0/B16 source-feature comparison is a second axis and must not be presented as evidence that a particular feature is novel solely because its contribution persists at one K.

- Liu et al., [FineCops-Ref](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14750), EMNLP 2024 main. Selected-body reading: §§3.1–3.4, dataset tables, and evaluation/analysis headings; it motivates this benchmark choice for composition, not reliability novelty.
- Wang et al., [SafeGround](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02419), v2; body reading: uncertainty definitions, calibration and FDR rule, and sample-count sensitivity.
- Kim and coauthors, [Zoom Consistency](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15376); body reading: crop geometry/formula, correctness AUC table, and application analysis.
